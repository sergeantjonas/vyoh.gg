## Naming

Tab label **Sessions**, route `/steam/sessions`, matching `SteamPlaySession`. "Sitting" was the working name and appears nowhere in user-facing copy; where this note still says sitting, read session.

## Premise

The api has recorded every observed Steam launch since 2026-05-16 as a `SteamPlaySession` row, and every achievement unlock carries Steam's own `unlocktime`. Today those rows feed exactly one surface: the five-bucket duration histogram on the recap's rhythm band. Everything about *when* the owner plays, *how long a sitting runs*, and *what happened inside it* is sitting in Postgres unread. This page reads it.

The framing the owner set on 2026-09-14: unlocks are one signal among several, not the subject. A sitting with zero unlocks is not a placeholder; it is a sitting whose headline comes from a different axis. Nothing on this page ever renders "no unlocks this session".

## Surface decision

A seventh Steam tab, `/steam/sessions`, sibling to `/steam/portrait`. The existing six are Profile, Portrait, Library, Wishlist, Upcoming and Achievements, and none of them is about time. The portrait says who the owner is when they play; this page says what their evenings look like.

The `/steam` landing carries a single **last sitting** card linking in, per the one-curated-highlight rule in [repo-conventions.md](../../repo-conventions.md) § "Per-stream routes". Nothing about sittings goes on `/` except through the recap's cross-stream ranking (chunk 6, optional).

Every read on this page names a game, so the whole endpoint is viewer-scoped on both sides per [hidden-games.md](hidden-games.md): a hidden game's sessions drop out for a visitor, and aggregates (the heatmap, the window count, rank denominators, the first-observed date) still count them anonymously. Chunk 2 implements that split by running the session query unfiltered and projecting in the service, while the unlock query is filtered at the database because every unlock row names its game. A hidden *neighbour* is nulled before the beat pass, since `bounced-from` / `moved-on-to` would otherwise carry its name.

## The beat model

A sitting's headline is chosen, not templated. Each signal produces zero or one **beat** with a strength; the strongest becomes the hero prose, the next one or two become supporting chips. This is a pure pass in `packages/shared/src/steam/sessions/` (`selectSessionBeats` in `beats.ts`), sibling in spirit to `recap-scoring.ts`, and it is the reason the page has no boring branch.

Signals, all from existing tables:

| Signal | Source | Example copy |
|---|---|---|
| Duration + clock | `startedAt`, `endedAt`, Brussels | "A past-midnight run, 22:40 to 02:52" |
| Rank within game | other rows for the appid | "Third-longest Onimusha sitting of 14" |
| Rank within window | rows in the last N weeks | "Longest sitting in five weeks" |
| Cadence | gap to previous sitting, same appid | "First Cyberpunk since June" / "Fourth evening in a row" |
| Playtime milestone | `SteamPlaytimeSnapshot` delta across the sitting | "Elden Ring passed 400 hours tonight" |
| Share of lifetime | duration ÷ `playtimeForever` | "3h, all of them" for a first sitting |
| Neighbours | adjacent session rows | "Opened after 8 minutes of Wallpaper Engine" |
| Completion state | `SteamGameCompletion` | "Still 14 out" / "Nothing left to unlock, and still back" |
| Unlocks + rarity | `SteamPlayerUnlock` × `SteamAchievementGlobalRarity` | "Three unlocks, rarest *Blademaster* at 2.3%" |
| Usual slot | the heatmap cell this sitting lands in | "A Tuesday, in the usual slot" |

Absence is never a beat. "No unlocks" scores nothing; a five-hour dry sitting gets "longest in five weeks" and the unlock row simply isn't drawn. The fallback when every axis is quiet is the sitting's own shape: "Two hours of Nightreign, a Tuesday, in the usual slot", and "usual slot" is a real claim because the heatmap earns it.

Strengths are relative, not absolute: rank beats score by how far from the median they sit, cadence by the log of the gap, milestones by the roundness of the number crossed. Pin the weights in one file with a fixture per beat kind, the way `recap-scoring.ts` does.

## Page composition

Editorial hero band, then dense bands below, the shape the portrait's layout pass landed on ([subject-chapter-design-spec.md](../cross-cutting/subject-chapter-design-spec.md) applies).

1. **Hero: the last sitting.** Game art backdrop, duration as the masthead, the winning beat as prose, supporting beats as chips. Unlock icons cascade in as a row *only when present*, rarity under each, hidden achievements masked as `???` per the S4 decision. Calm: the duration does not count up.
2. **Sitting timeline.** Weeks as rows, hours as columns; each sitting a bar positioned by start hour and sized by duration, coloured by game accent, unlock dots pinned at their exact `unlockedAt` inside the bar. Hover a bar for the digest tooltip. A brush along the bottom scrubs the window. **visx**, because brush + timeline is exactly what visx is reserved for; plain SVG stays for the dot and line overlays.
3. **Hour-of-day heatmap.** 7 × 24 minutes-played per weekday-hour, Brussels time. The Steam sibling of the LoL chronotype; reuse the death-matchup heatmap plumbing. Unlock-free by nature.
4. **Per-game rhythm strips.** Small multiples: one strip per game in the window, sitting lengths as bars over time, unlocks as optional ticks. A game with no achievements at all still gets a strip. Plain SVG.
5. **Playtime milestones.** Which sittings pushed a game past 10, 50, 100, 500 hours. Pure duration data.
6. **Records chips.** Longest sitting, latest finish, most unlocks in one sitting, longest dry spell, quickest bounce. Each names the game and links to `/steam/library/$appid`. This is where the portrait's cards 4 and 8 land.
7. **Off-camera ledger.** Unlocks with no matching sitting, grouped by game and day, captioned "unlocked while the api wasn't watching", with the count. Turns the coverage caveat into a feature instead of a lie. Every aggregate above carries a "based on N sittings since <first observed>" footnote.

## Data shape

One endpoint, one type, one query:

```ts
// packages/shared/src/steam/sessions/sessions.ts
export type SteamSittings = {
  window: { from: string; to: string; observedSince: string; sittingCount: number };
  sittings: SteamSitting[];          // newest first; each carries its beats + unlocks
  hourMatrix: number[][];            // 7 × 24 minutes, Brussels
  perGame: SteamSittingGameStrip[];  // appid, name, accent, sittings[], unlockTicks[]
  milestones: SteamPlaytimeMilestone[];
  records: SteamSittingRecords;
  offCamera: SteamOffCameraUnlockGroup[];
};
```

`GET /api/steam/sessions?weeks=N` behind `@WithViewer()` + `@ViewerIsOwner()`, service takes the curation sets as an argument, web key ends in `viewerScope(isOwner)`, fetch sends `credentials: "include"`. The loader primes the *public* key; the page reads only our Postgres so it qualifies for SSR priming the same way the portrait did. **Measured 2026-09-14 against the dev api at 12 weeks: 89 kB in 33–42 ms over three runs, 41 sessions, 8 game strips, 2 milestones, 21 off-camera groups.** The bulk is the unlock rows (description and icon per unlock, once in the digest and once more in the ledger). That is 25× the portrait's payload and clears the SSR latency question but not the size one; chunk 3 decides whether the hero primes alone (a `weeks=1` read) with the rest fetched on the client, or whether unlock descriptions leave the payload.

The unlock join: `unlockedAt BETWEEN startedAt - 4 min AND endedAt + 4 min`, same appid. The slack is `SESSION_UNLOCK_SLACK_MS` in `unlocks-within.ts`, with the boundary pinned in its test.

## Live session (owner idea, 2026-09-15)

While a game is open, the hero should be the *current* session: "Now playing · Onimusha" as the eyebrow, a duration counting up from the open row's `startedAt` as the masthead, and the beats the session has already earned as chips (a streak or a return is known at launch; unlocks arrive with the event-driven refresh on close, so mid-session the unlock row stays empty and honest). This answers open decision 3 below and replaces the plan to defer to the now-playing strip. Needs: the api to carry `live: { game, startedAt } | null` from the open `SteamPlaySession` row (today the service drops open rows), the web hook's `staleTime` to shorten while `live` is set, and a minute-resolution ticker — the number moves, it does not spin; per the calm-aesthetic rule the masthead re-renders once a minute with a soft pulse on the eyebrow dot rather than counting seconds. Scoped as **chunk 3b**, ahead of the timeline, because it is the page's most visible moment and the cheapest remaining one.

✅ **Done 2026-09-15.** `SteamSessions.live` carries the open row with `selectLiveSessionBeats()` — the pass filtered to the seven kinds known at launch, run over a matrix that includes the running session so the slot arithmetic stays sound. Review added the guard the first cut lacked: an open row is live only if the player-state poll is within fifteen minutes (`LIVE_POLL_FRESH_MS`) **and** still shows that game, because the row closes on the tick that sees the game gone and no tick comes while the api is down. On the web, `useElapsedMinutes` re-renders on the elapsed-minute boundary (not a wall-clock interval, so the number steps exactly when it changes), reads the clock on first render because the route is not server-primed, and the hook polls the endpoint every two minutes only while `live` is set. The now-playing strip on `/` reads `SteamPlayerState`, not this row, so the two *can* momentarily disagree by one tick; the freshness gate keeps that to a tick rather than an outage.

Web-only, no api change; it needs three closed sessions of the game before it speaks. The other candidates (usual finish for the slot, mid-session unlock refresh) stay parked.

**Owner review, 2026-09-15: "a line with a small blip — could be more eye-catching."** Three changes, all on the existing vocabulary rather than new decoration. The Steam tab strip now carries the same red `Live` chip the LoL section shows for a live match, reworded `Playing` and pointed at `/steam/sessions`, so every Steam tab says a game is open (`SectionLiveTab` gained `label` and `ariaLabel`; the chip reads the presence poll, the same source the hero's live state is gated on). On the strip, the live bar breathes and the now-line gained a `now` label. No new colour, no confetti; the calm-aesthetic rule holds. Two things review caught: a `[animation-duration:2.4s]` variant beside `motion-safe:animate-ping` is overridden by the later `animation` shorthand, so the ping blinked at one second — both animations now use the arbitrary shorthand with the duration inside it; and the chip belongs in the shell's `leading` slot beside the identity, because the seven-tab row has no room and the `live` slot wrapped it onto a third row. The live variant's compositor cost is unmeasured: the probe cannot open a game, and the two infinite opacity animations run only while one is.

**Owner review, 2026-09-16: "I started playing a game but it doesn't show until I refresh."** The sessions query polled only while `live` was already set, so a launch with the page idle waited for the five-minute stale window or a reload. Rather than poll the 45 kB payload continuously, `useSessionsPresenceSync()` rides the 30-second player-state poll that `PresenceMounts` already keeps warm at the root for the app's lifetime, and invalidates the `["steam", "sessions"]` prefix whenever the current appid changes — open, switch or close. A mounted hero refetches at once; an unmounted landing chip is served stale on its next mount instead of from cache. Two guards: the first answer after mount carries no observed transition, so it is only recorded; and a change that arrives together with a viewer-scope flip is the key changing, not the game, because a hidden game projects to no game publicly and `keepPreviousData` carries the public answer through hydration. Root-mounted rather than in the Steam section so a launch seen on `/` still marks the entry stale before the owner navigates in. Safe against the poller's write order: `recordTransition()` opens the row before `steamPlayerState` is upserted, so a client that has seen the new game will find the open row on the refetch.

**Owner review, 2026-09-16: the off-camera ledger "looks very unorganised."** Three causes, all layout: the icon column was as wide as its sample, so one-, two- and three-icon rows started their text at three different x positions; the rows came newest-day-first across games, so one game's name repeated and truncated five times; and the date and count shared a ragged right column. The ledger now groups the shown rows by game in first-appearance order, with the name and the game's total as a header once, a fixed-width icon column, the day and count on one baseline and the sample names on a line of their own beneath, where they get the card's full width instead of two words and an ellipsis. The six-row cap still counts game-days, and the trailer says *days* rather than *rows*.

**Owner review, 2026-09-16: a `0m · 02:14 to 02:14` hero.** The row behind it opened at 00:14:49.162Z and closed three milliseconds later, off the two-minute boundary; Steam's presence then never reported the game again and showed the owner offline from 00:28Z, so whatever was being played was not being broadcast by Steam. The mechanism for two transitions inside one instant is not established — only the cron and the boot backfill call the sync, the registry guards overlap, one api process was running and no other session ran anything at that time; the dev api's terminal at 02:14 local is the remaining evidence. What is established is that closing a session anchors `endedAt` to the last tick that still saw the game, so a game seen on one tick alone reads as zero minutes, and Steam presence does flicker. `SESSION_MIN_MINUTES` / `isBlipSession()` / `excludeBlipSessions()` in the shared beat module now name that floor once, and the sessions service iterates the helper over the closed rows before anything reads them — hero, streak and neighbour beats, strip, records, matrix and ledger alike — which retired the record-only `BOUNCE_MIN_MINUTES` floor. An unlock that landed inside a floored row now reads as off-camera, which is the honest reading: a single tick cannot claim it. The other readers of `SteamPlaySession` (the `home-*` services, `steam-moments`, the portrait) still aggregate raw rows; a zero-minute row is harmless to a sum and a day split, so they are a follow-up in [open-work.md](../open-work.md), not part of this change. The 3 ms row is to be deleted by hand (the session's permission classifier refused the `DELETE`). **Closed 2026-09-25:** all six iterate the helper, which takes an open row and keeps it. "Harmless" was wrong in two places and nearly so in a third: the portrait counted a flicker as a launch day toward the engagement rescue, the recap's first-time receipt could open on a flicker as the first sit-down and count it in `sessionCount`, and the day split's one-minute floor counted even the 3 ms row as a minute.

**Owner review, 2026-09-18: the landing chip "is very boring and empty compared to the next."** Two causes, and only one of them was content. The card sat in `/steam`'s stretch grid beside the five-row unlocks chip, and `CardShell`'s `comfortable` density pins the prescription with `mt-auto`, so the row's slack collected between the verdict and the footnote — the hole the density comment already predicted for the compact band, at a size the comfortable one makes worse. `CardShell` gained an opt-in `evidenceFills` that hands the slack to the evidence slot instead; it is opt-in because a card whose evidence is a fixed list would only move the same gap inside its own wrapper. The content half is that the chip already fetches the whole four-week payload and rendered three lines of it, so both additions are web-only with no api change: [session-day-strip.tsx](../../../apps/web/src/steam/sessions/session-day-strip.tsx), one bar per owner-local day over the window, and [recent-session-rows.tsx](../../../apps/web/src/steam/sessions/recent-session-rows.tsx), the five newest sessions with a duration bar scaled to the longest of them. `buildDayStrip()` in `packages/shared/src/steam/sessions/day-strip.ts` does the bucketing, splitting a midnight-spanning session across both days the way `buildHourMatrix` splits it across hours, and iterating `excludeBlipSessions()` like every other reader.

Three things the owner's passes corrected, all of them the same failure — a short form that dropped the noun its long form carried:

- **One hue claimed the whole window.** Under a headline naming Onimusha, a single-tone strip read as all-Onimusha when a fifth of the window was Mortal Shell II. Cells now carry `byGame`, and a bar is two-toned: the headline's game in the accent, everything else muted, with a legend, per-day tooltips naming each game and its minutes, and the games in the sr-only table so colour never states the fact alone. Deliberately **not** a hue per game — a categorical palette would need validating against a set that changes every time the owner starts something new, to answer what the tooltip answers by name.
- **The receipt line read as an unexplained statistic.** `Onimusha: Way of the Sword · 1h 14m` was the last session's length, but with the rows below carrying the same minute it had nothing left to say and the owner read it as a median. Gone for a finished session; while a game is open it becomes `Playing for 1h 30m`, the one case the rows cannot cover because a running session has no end yet.
- **`2 to finish` named no noun.** The `nearly-complete` chip is two *achievements* from 100%, which its sentence form says and its chip form did not — it read as sessions or games. Now `2 achievements left`, pinned by a test, since the chip is the form that appears with no sentence beside it.

Repeated identical capsule art on the rows was replaced by the per-row duration bar on the owner's call: four rows of the same thumbnail carry no information, where a bar differs per row and echoes the strip above it. The rows then lost their link too — they pointed at the game's library entry, which promises a session detail view the app does not have, and the card already carries one link to where sessions live.

**Review caught a real DST defect in the first cut of `buildDayStrip`.** The window was built by walking the instant back in 24 h steps and re-formatting each one, with a comment claiming that was what made it transition-safe. It is the opposite: a local day either side of a transition is 23 or 25 hours long, so at `through` = `2026-03-29T22:30Z` the walk skips 29 March entirely — every minute played that day resolves to a cell that does not exist and vanishes from the bars, the peak and the sr-only table — and across a fall-back it yields 27 cells for a 28-day window, making the `aria-label` a lie. The window now resolves the local day once and counts back on UTC dates anchored at midday, well clear of both boundaries, with a fixture per transition at the instants that reproduced it. Two smaller things from the same pass: the two tone heights rounded independently and could total 99 % or 101 %, so one is now derived from the other; and minutes were rounded per hour slice and summed, which could put a day's tooltip a minute away from the `durationMinutes` the row beneath it reads, so the cell accumulates milliseconds and rounds once.

Known and accepted: the bar tooltips are pointer-only — `Trigger asChild` on a `div` adds no tab stop — because 28 tab stops in a landing chip is worse than none, and the `ChartDataTable` beneath already carries the day, the minutes, the games and the unlocks.

**Chunk 0 amendment, 2026-09-15.** The 29-hour row was split along its unlock clusters rather than deleted, on the owner's call: `cmsz31xfw0004d5nds7lrrxa1` now ends 2026-08-19 00:35Z (4 h 39 m, five unlocks), and two new rows cover 08-19 14:45 → 18:20Z (3 h 35 m, two unlocks) and 08-19 21:30 → 08-20 01:16Z (3 h 46 m, three unlocks). Twelve hours against the snapshots' ten-and-a-bit; the boundaries are estimates and the note is their only record. The `4h+` bucket reads 3 again, all of them real.
